## 10.6 Summary

The main concepts and results of this chapter are listed below: $\bullet$  Iterative methods. Splitting $A$ as $A = M$ - $N. \bullet$  Convergence of a sequence of vectors or matrices. $\bullet  A$ criterion for the convergence of the sequence $(B^{k})$ of powers of a matrix $B$ to zero in terms of the spectral radius $\rho (B). \bullet  A$ characterization of the spectral radius $\rho (B)$ as the limit of the sequence $(\|B^{k}\|^{1/k}). \bullet  A$ criterion of the convergence of iterative methods. $\bullet$  Asymptotic behavior of iterative methods. $\bullet$  Splitting $A$ as $A =$ D-E-F, and the methods of Jacobi, Gauss–Seidel, and relaxation (and SOR). $\bullet$  The Jacobi matrix, $J = D^{-1}(E + F). \bullet$  The Gauss–Seidel matrix, $\mathcal{L}_{1} = (D$ - $E)^{-1}F. \bullet$  The matrix of relaxation, $\mathcal{L}_{\omega} = (D$ - $\omega E)^{-1}((1$ - $\omega )D + \omega F). \bullet$  Convergence of iterative methods: a general result when $A = M$ - $N$ is Hermitian positive definite. $\bullet  A$ sufficient condition for the convergence of the methods of Jacobi, Gauss–Seidel, and relaxation. The Ostrowski-Reich theorem: $A$ is Hermitian positive definite and $\omega  \in (0, 2). \bullet  A$ necessary condition for the convergence of the methods of Jacobi , Gauss–Seidel, and relaxation: $\omega  \in (0, 2). \bullet$  The case of tridiagonal matrices (possibly by blocks). Simultaneous convergence or divergence of Jacobi’s method and Gauss–Seidel’s method, and comparison of the spectral radii of $\rho (J)$ and $\rho (\mathcal{L}_{1}): \rho (\mathcal{L}_{1}) = (\rho (J))^{2}. \bullet$  The case of tridiagonal Hermitian positive definite matrices (possibly by blocks). The methods of Jacobi, Gauss–Seidel, and relaxation, all converge. $\bullet$  In the above case, there is a unique optimal relaxation parameter for which $\rho (\mathcal{L}_{\omega0}) < \rho (\mathcal{L}_{1}) = (\rho (J))^{2} < \rho (J)$ (if $\rho (J) \neq  0)$.
