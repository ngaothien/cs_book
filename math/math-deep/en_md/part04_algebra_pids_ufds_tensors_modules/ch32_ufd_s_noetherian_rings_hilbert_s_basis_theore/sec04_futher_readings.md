Remark: Only a small part of Lemma 32.21 was used in the above proof, namely, the fact that $L_{i}(\mathfrak{A})$ is an ideal. A shorter proof of Theorem 32.23 making full use of Lemma 32.21 can be given as follows:

*Proof.* (Second proof) Let $(\mathfrak{A}_{i})_{i\ge1}$ be an ascending sequence of ideals in A[X]. Consider the doubly indexed family $(L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{j}))$ of ideals in $A$. Since $A$ is noetherian, by the maximal property, this family has a maximal element $L_{p}(\mathfrak{A}_{q})$. Since the $L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{j})’s$ form an ascending sequence when either $i$ or $j$ is fixed, we have $L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{j}) = L_{p}(\mathfrak{A}_{q})$ for all $i$ and $j$ with $i \ge p$ and $j \ge q$, and thus, $L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{q}) = L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{j})$ for all $i$ and $j$ with $i \ge p$ and $j \ge q$. On the other hand, for any fixed $i$, the a.c.c. shows that there exists some integer n(i) so that $L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{j}) = L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{n(i)})$ for all $j \ge$ n(i). Since $L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{q}) = L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{j})$ when $i \ge p$ and $j \ge q$, we may take n(i) $= q$ if $i \ge p$. This shows that there is some $n_{0}$ so that n(i) $\le n_{0}$ for all $i \ge 0$, and thus, we have $L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{j}) = L_{i}(\mathfrak{A}_{n(0)})$ for every $i$ and for every $j \ge$ n(0). By Lemma 32.21, we get $\mathfrak{A}_{j} = \mathfrak{A}_{n(0)}$ for every $j \ge$ n(0), establishing the fact that A[X] satisfies the a.c.c. Using induction, we immediately obtain the following important result.

> **Corollary 32.23.** If $A$ is a noetherian ring, then $A[X_{1}, \dots  , X_{n}]$ is also a noetherian ring. Since a field $K$ is obviously noetherian (since it has only two ideals, $(0)$ and $K)$, we also have:

> **Corollary 32.24.** If $K$ is a field, then $K[X_{1}, \dots  , X_{n}]$ is a noetherian ring.
